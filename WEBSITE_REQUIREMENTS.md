# Defining Website Requirements Around an Established Business
*Phillip J. Pirages Fine Books & Manuscripts*

A website redesign at Pirages required consideration of the records and working practices that supported the business. Inventory descriptions, photographs, customer histories, catalogue mailing lists, consignments, and financial reporting all depended on information maintained in the office database.

## Translating daily work into requirements

I documented existing processes and identified questions that a proposed system would need to address. My consolidated notes from July 2009 covered data organization, business rules, customer needs, website navigation, and questions for the developer.

The requirements reflected the business’s particular circumstances. Customers could have seasonal addresses, institutions required careful mailing-list management, and staff needed to retrieve information in combinations that could not all be specified in advance. Phil’s preferences also shaped the proposed customer experience, including prominent contact information and an optional login.

## Working with colleagues

In June 2010, I circulated an outline to Jenny Johns and Tammy Opheim for their additions before preparing a communication to the website provider. Their knowledge of customer service, financial reporting, and office procedures helped make the requirements more concrete.

Together, the correspondence addressed questions such as:

- Could staff create queries and reports as new needs arose?
- Could customer and order histories remain accessible?
- How would consignment reporting and partially sold groups of items be handled?
- Could the office retain a local data copy for outages and batch processing?
- How would online customer changes affect the catalogue mailing list?

My role included organizing these concerns, researching technical implications, and helping translate between office practices and the proposed technology.

## Investigating the proposed system

I examined how the proposed Magento platform might accommodate those requirements. A revised document shows my thinking developing: an initial concern about access permissions became a more specific question about working with its entity–attribute–value database structure.

The evaluation considered both the customer-facing website and the staff’s ability to continue managing the underlying information. It also raised questions about customization, provider capabilities, and the scope of the work being purchased.

## Continuing the investigation

By March 2014, my notes recorded further consideration of electronic call forms and linked customer and item records. They also documented contact with Bibliopolis about a broader database solution and a referral to the developer of BookHound.

The surviving correspondence establishes the requirements analysis, collaborative review, and technical investigation. It does not establish that every proposal was implemented or provide a complete account of the eventual system transition.

**Skills demonstrated:** business analysis, requirements gathering, workflow assessment, technical research, and collaboration with colleagues and service providers.
